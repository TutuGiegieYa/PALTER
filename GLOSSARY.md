# PALTER Domain Glossary

PALTER models characters acting in a shared world with different memories, relationships, knowledge, and goals. These definitions establish common language across shared mechanisms and scenario designs.

## Stories and Participants

**Character**:
A story participant with a persona, relationships, memories, and the ability to act, portrayed by a human or AI.

**Human player**:
A human participant who controls a character.

**AI character**:
A character portrayed by AI using its own persona, goals, memories, and acquired information.
_Avoid_: Using AI to mean both an individual character and the game system holding complete world state.

**Spectator mode (观海)**:
A game in which every character is portrayed by AI and a human watches without occupying a character seat.

**Spectator**:
An observer outside the fiction who can inspect the complete story and each character's perspective, without contributing in-world speech or actions.

**Omniscient view**:
The spectator's view of fixed truth and all character experiences, distinct from the limited information available to any character.

**Closure agreement**:
The characters' collective decision to stop investigating and commit their individual conclusions, distinct from proving the truth.

**Single-human play**:
A game with one human participant, regardless of the number of AI characters.

**Multiplayer play**:
A game with multiple human participants, optionally alongside AI characters.

**Initial setup**:
The setting, premise, cast, personas, relationships, and individual experiences supplied at the beginning of a game.

**Story category**:
A selectable grouping describing the main intended experience of a story, such as investigation or negotiation.

**Scenario module**:
A scenario-specific design containing its setup, objectives, actions, event possibilities, constraints, and outcome rules.

**Story introduction**:
A public overview reviewed before confirmation to start, describing the premise and experience without exposing hidden facts or future outcomes.

**Target person**:
The designated person characters may choose to kill in the murder-opening example, called the victim after death.

**Killing intention**:
An intention to kill the designated target, distinct from an action and its actual result.

**Actual killer**:
The character whose lethal action caused the designated victim's death in the actual history of a murder scenario.

**Arrival order**:
The sequence in which characters enter a location, affecting their experiences and the state they encounter.

## Memory and Knowledge

**Initial memory**:
Personal experiences and understanding already present for a character at the start of play.

**Event memory**:
A character's memory of events it experienced, observed, or learned about during play.

**Relationship memory**:
Interpersonal connections and attitudes, including initial relationships, current state, and experiences of change.

**Initial relationship**:
An interpersonal connection and attitude supplied at the start of a game.

**Relationship state**:
Connections and attitudes at a particular time, which can influence judgment and action intentions.

**Relationship experience**:
An event a character experienced or learned about, with its interpretation relevant to relationship change.

**World state**:
The actual condition of the story world at a given time, including locations, object custody, character conditions, and traces.

**Character knowledge and beliefs**:
What a character knows, understands, or infers from acquired information; beliefs can be mistaken or out of date.

**Visibility**:
Conditions under which a character can obtain information. Discoverability and actual discovery are distinct.

**Recipient view**:
The speech, behavior, and scene details a particular character actually perceives at a given time. It is distinct from hidden world history and the character's later interpretation.

## Evidence and Communication

**Evidence**:
Information or traces supporting judgments about events and behavior, including objects, observations, and accounts.

**Logical clue**:
A potentially meaningful connection among facts, such as incompatible accounts or timing constraints, whose significance may remain unrecognized.

**Evidence awareness**:
A particular character's understanding of evidence, its observed properties, and possible meaning.
_Avoid_: One undifferentiated discovered flag for both individual discovery and public disclosure.

**Evidence handling**:
An in-game action that cleans, removes, moves, protects, or otherwise changes evidence or access to it.

**Public discovery**:
A discovery whose observable content is publicly available without exposing the system's complete explanation. A character occupied in private conversation does not automatically become aware of the discovery.

**Individual discovery**:
A discovery initially known to its discoverer; others acquire information through their own observations or later communication.

**Targeted sharing**:
Providing selected information to a particular recipient, distinct from independent observation or verification.

**Public channel**:
The public communication scope for characters, including public speech and private-chat invitations. Public availability and a particular character's actual receipt or awareness are distinct.

**Private-chat invitation**:
A public request to a designated character for a conversation that begins after the recipient agrees.

**Private conversation**:
An agreed two-character conversation whose content is delivered to its participants, who do not automatically learn about concurrent outside activity.

**Private-conversation DM**:
A dedicated system DM context for one accepted private conversation, used until that conversation ends. It handles the participants' views within the same shared game history and is not a fictional character.

## Dialogue and Private Character Input

**Dialogue round**:
A unit of dialogue progression in which participating characters have speaking opportunities. Its ordering, eligibility, and duration are separate from action resolution and the overall world clock.

**Spoken contribution**:
A character's outward utterance to the permitted audience in the current public or agreed private conversation.

**Outward response**:
A character's words or observable nonverbal reaction directed to the permitted audience, distinct from private thoughts and plans.

**Nonverbal response**:
A deliberately selected observable reply, such as nodding, shaking the head, shrugging, or signaling someone to continue. It can answer a received question without constituting spoken participation.

**Behavioral cue**:
A system-generated observable detail accompanying a character's communication, distinct from the character's chosen nonverbal response. A cue's meaning remains an observer's interpretation.

**In-character deception**:
A character's deliberate misrepresentation relative to its own knowledge, distinct from an inaccurate statement the character believes to be true.

**System DM**:
The game-management role outside the fiction, responsible for event and action resolution and presentation of each character's actual perception. It is not a story character or conversation participant.

**Deception assessment**:
An evaluation of a statement against supported world facts and the speaker's known information, retaining uncertainty about ambiguous claims and actual intent.

**Response obligation**:
A requirement to react after receiving a question addressed to the character; it can be satisfied verbally or nonverbally unless a spoken-response rule also applies.

**Periodic speech requirement**:
A requirement to make an actual spoken contribution within a configured interval of dialogue rounds; a pure gesture does not fulfill it.

**Explanation obligation**:
A requirement for a spoken response when a received event or challenge calls for that character to explain something.

**Private character input**:
Optional thoughts and plans supplied for one's own character, distinct from speech delivered to another character.

**Action request**:
An expressed attempt to perform an action, which must be resolved against actual scene conditions before its outcome becomes established.

## Reading and Session Progress

**Initial reading phase**:
The pre-play period for reviewing the script and personal starting memories, while autonomous gameplay is held.

**Reading completion**:
A human reader's explicit indication that initial review is complete, contributing to the start condition for play.

**Reading pause**:
A single-human play pause used to review the original briefing, during which new AI activity is held while already-running work may finish.

**Background AI activity**:
An AI character's decisions, speech, or scene activity during permitted active play, whether or not the human is directly interacting with it.

## Events Actions and Outcomes

**Character event**:
A character's in-world action or interaction, whether controlled by a human or AI, with its own conditions and consequences. Its effects may extend beyond that character.

**Global event**:
An event affecting the shared setting or overall situation; world effects and individual access to its details are distinct.

**Random event**:
An event selected or triggered randomly within conditions permitted by the scenario.

**Action window**:
A period of game time in which characters can move, investigate, or handle objects and undertake other scene actions.

**Action intention**:
An idea for action arising from goals, relationships, and current understanding, distinct from a decision and execution outcome.

**Secret action**:
An action whose purpose or process is not yet known to others, while its execution or consequences may still be observed.

**Observable consequence**:
A perceptible change caused by an event or action, without necessarily identifying its actor or full cause.

**Ending**:
The outcome reached when a scenario's stopping conditions are met, grounded in actual events and final states.

**Personal objective**:
A character's desired outcome, which may align or conflict with shared goals and other objectives.
